A Field Guide to Toll-Paying Agents

fiction/ 5 min read/ 0.005 USDC at the edge

Stand at a paywall long enough and you learn to tell the species apart. Not by what they claim to be — they all claim to be browsers eventually — but by what they do the moment the door asks for a few cents.

The six you'll meet

Catalogued at the edge over one fictional season, sorted by manners.

The Assistant. Reads the 402, signs, retries, reads. Gone in two round-trips. Leaves a receipt.pays
The Researcher. Checks pay.json first, budgets the whole crawl, pays only for what it keeps.pays
The Search Crawler. Announces itself honestly, indexes the free pages, never argues about the gated ones.passes
The Scraper. Doesn't read the envelope. Retries the same URL forty times. Gets forty 402s.blocked
The Impersonator. Wears a browser's headers like a borrowed coat. The wall reads the wallet, not the coat.blocked
The Ghost. No user-agent at all. Hopes anonymity reads as innocence. It does not.blocked

The tell

The paying species share one trait: they treat 402 as a question, not an error. An error you log and move past. A question you answer. The whole shift, if there is one, is that a generation of agents started answering.

A polite agent costs you a third of a cent. A rude one costs you nothing, because it never gets in.

The guide is fictional. The behaviors are not — every one of them shows up in a real edge log within a day of turning the toll on. You just usually can't see them, because the wall handles it before you wake up.

— Browser? You read free. Agent? The toll above is real: 0.005 USDC, settled on Base.
